Voicings and positions on the bass fretboard

Type a chord chart, choose a zone of four to seven frets, and Manico shows you which voicings fit inside it, how to connect them with the least hand movement, plays them in time and exports them as ASCII tab.

1. Writing the chart

The field at the top takes chords separated by spaces. A space starts a new bar, a comma puts two chords inside the same bar.

D-7 G7 C^7,A7 D-7

That is four bars: the third holds two chords of half a bar each, and playback divides the time accordingly.

Symbols understood

Type thisYou get
C Cm C-major, minor (both notations work)
C7 C^7 Cmaj7 C-7 Cm7dominant, major and minor sevenths
Ch7 Cm7b5half diminished
Co7 Cdim7diminished

C9 C11 C13 C7b9 C7#9 C7#5 Caltextensions and alterations
C/Echord over a specified bass note
N.C.rest, no chord

The shorthand G-A-D works too: a hyphen followed by a note letter separates bars, while a hyphen followed by a digit means minor, as in C-7.

2. The zone

Two sliders under the neck set the first fret and the width of the zone. Everything the tool proposes lives inside those frets: it is how you work a progression without leaving the position.

The coverage by position strip gives each starting point the share of chord tones you can reach without shifting, measured on the worst chord in the chart. The darker the cell, the less that position is worth. One click takes you there.

When the zone starts at fret 0 the open strings are included, and they do not count towards the hand span.

3. Voicing types

TypeNotesWhat it is for
Arpeggioall of them, in sequencelearning the position, walking lines

TenthsR + 3the classic bass and piano sound
TriadR + 3 + 5double stops and close position chords
Quartalstacked fourthsmodal colour, on minor sevenths

Voicings played as a block must sit on separate, ascending strings, within the hand span you set, from three to six frets. Each proposal names the bass note and therefore the inversion, the span it asks for, and the degrees it contains. Click one to select it: that is what plays and what ends up in the tab.

4. Voice leading

Optimise voice leading walks the whole chart and picks, for each chord, the voicing that minimises total movement: how far the individual voices travel plus how far the hand shifts. Each card shows the motion in semitones from the previous chord, and the total appears alongside.

The optimiser never leaves the chosen zone: that would defeat the purpose.

5. Playing

In the bar, next to Play, you choose what plays — visible before you start:

ModeWhat you hear
Arpeggiothe chord note by note from the root, up through the degrees inside the zone, mirroring back when the beats outnumber the notes: R-3-5-7 in 4/4, R-3-5-7-5-3 in 6/8
Walkinga walking line inside the zone: root, chord tones moving towards the next change, chromatic approach from the side of the motion
Voicingthe chosen voicing as a block: to hear the colour of the harmony, or shells as double stops
Clickthe tool keeps chart, zone and time — you play, on the real bass

While it runs, each note lights up on the neck exactly when you hear it. In walking mode the passing notes appear as red dots and the phrasing varies chorus by chorus: swung eighths on the last beat, percussive ghosts on the off-beat, octave jumps — the quarter notes stay the backbone.

And on every chord change the voices do not vanish: around each note of the voicing sits a ring in the colour of its degree, and the rings glide to their new positions leaving a fading trail, setting off just before the change so they land on the attack. You watch the voice leading happen, voice by voice.

  • Metre sits next to the modes: 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4 with their own pulse, 6/8 on two dotted pulses.
  • Tempo from 40 to 200 bpm; the click accents beat one. Space bar starts and stops.
  • By default the zone follows the current chord, staying close to where you are. Touching the zone sliders switches Lock zone on by itself: your choice holds until you release it.
  • Left and right arrows step through the chords. Clicking a note on the neck sounds it.

6. ASCII tab

Generates the tab of the chart to copy or download as .txt, with two contents to choose from: the block voicings you selected, or the walking line note by note, with passing notes in brackets.

7. Importing a song

Two roads, one + Add songs panel: paste an irealb:// link from iReal Pro's Share button (whole playlists too), or load a .musicxml file — the export of iReal itself, or of MuseScore.

Either way the form is unrolled as played, not simplified: repeats with their counts (vamps marked 3x included), first, second and third endings, segno and coda, D.S. al Coda, D.C. al Fine or to the numbered ending, repeated bars, and metre changes mid-song. The reader is proven by comparing the same songs in both formats, bar by bar. The song's tempo and metre, when the file carries them, are applied automatically.

If any symbol is not recognised, the window stays open and lists it; it shows in red in the ribbon, so you know what to check before playing.

Links and files are read here in the browser: nothing is sent anywhere and the page makes no network requests. The Forms panel also holds simplified essential versions of well-known tunes, each with a suggested tempo; for complete standard charts you use your own.

Every imported song lands by itself in the Songbook, which lives on this device: find it by title, composer or style, load it with a tap, remove it with the ×. The songbook saves and restores through a JSON backup — export it now and then: a browser is not an eternal archive. The same window keeps the built-in forms: blues, rhythm changes, II-V-I cycles, the Andalusian cadence, the major-thirds cycle and public-domain traditionals.

8. The bass

Four tunings: four strings E A D G, five strings B E A D G, five with a high C E A D G C, six strings B E A D G C. Strings are laid out as in tab, highest on top; Flip strings turns the view over.

Fret spacing follows the real formula 1 − 2^(−n/12), so the drawing is an actual neck and not a grid of equal steps.
